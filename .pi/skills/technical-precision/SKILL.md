# Precise technical expression

Build a stable grid with aligned labels, predictable section spacing and a clear
separation between overview, comparison and detail. Give model names, units and
conditions consistent typographic roles. Use tabular numerals when they improve
comparison, with a readable system-font hierarchy rather than a decorative
monospace treatment for all text.

Let real product photography, supported diagrams and carefully labeled tables carry
technical character. Align image proportions and captions across comparable items;
retain the whole component when a crop would hide identifying details. Use restrained
rules and spacing to group information; distinguish data panels from navigation.
A compact page may use moderate density, but keep qualifications and mobile table
relationships readable.

Apply Designer's color-evidence rules. This direction
requires neither dark backgrounds nor blue, neon accents, gradients or fictitious
telemetry. Use selective emphasis to identify the main action and important differences.
If the material is primarily narrative, borrow grid discipline without turning it
into a dashboard. Do not invent performance, precision or safety claims to support
the aesthetic.

Keep native interactions direct, with clear focus and active states. Avoid decorative
movement that competes with reading specifications. Combine with product-showcase
or data-storytelling only when their content methods are actually needed. Follow
the Designer's existing submission and validation contract; no extra output or pass.
