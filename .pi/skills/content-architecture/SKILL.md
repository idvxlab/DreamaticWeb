# Source material to page structure

Start from what a visitor must understand, compare or do. Group supplied material
by those questions rather than reproducing PDF pages or presentation slide order.
Separate primary information, supporting proof, detailed lookup and optional context.
Combine repeated themes without merging distinct offerings or losing qualifications.
For a small amount of content, a short page may be sufficient; do not manufacture
sections to fill a template.

Analyst recommends sections with a clear purpose and evidence-based content.
Identify the main message, essential details and supported action for each section.
Keep detailed parameters available near the relevant claim instead of placing every
number in a hero. Surface missing information through the existing limitations
field; do not fill a conventional testimonials, statistics or client-logo section
with invented material.

Designer chooses the final ordering, navigation and amount of progressive disclosure.
Use headings that describe the content, keep labels stable across navigation and
sections, and avoid competing primary actions. A contact link, downloadable material
or section anchor must correspond to an available destination or capability.

Rewrite only where the request permits. Preserve approved copy, names, terminology,
numeric conditions and meaningful uncertainty. Do not convert aspirations into
promises or remove a qualifier to make a shorter headline. For local revisions keep
unaffected content/order intact; restructure more broadly only when the scope allows.
When bilingual content is requested, preserve meaning and define a consistent primary
language rather than adding decorative English fragments.

Use already supplied analysis and targeted source reads; no new interview or research
pass is required. Follow the owning role's existing output contract. Use
product-showcase or data-storytelling only for substantive product/data decisions.
