# Expressive brand composition

Choose one strong focal device: scale contrast, a distinctive crop, typographic
composition or a brand-derived graphic motif. Carry it through a few repeated
relationships rather than applying every expressive effect at once. Establish a
recognizable opening, then alternate visual emphasis with quieter reading sections.
Use asymmetry only while navigation and reading order remain clear.

Use available brand imagery purposefully. Protect subjects, logos and product identity
when cropping; avoid putting essential text over an unpredictable image region.
When imagery is weak or unavailable, use typography, spacing and supported CSS
shapes instead of invented visuals or empty image placeholders. Keep longer copy
at a comfortable measure; oversized headings must still wrap naturally on mobile.

Apply Designer's color-evidence rules. Expressive does not require high saturation, a particular hue, oversized logos or a fixed hero
layout. Reserve strong emphasis for the message or action that matters most;
secondary sections should not all compete as campaign posters. Keep claims grounded
in source content rather than manufacturing social proof or promotional promises.

Use clear native navigation and action labels. Avoid animation-dependent meaning,
script-dependent effects and controls that merely look interactive. Use Designer
revision-scope rules for existing pages and the normal submission and checks; do not add a brand workshop or separate style proposal step.
