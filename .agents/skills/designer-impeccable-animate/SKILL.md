# Give motion a thesis

Start from the selected visual world and visitor task. In Persuade or Experience mode, motion may carry the page's identity. In Operate or Read mode, prioritize feedback and continuity. A generic fade-and-rise, tiny hover lift or parallax layer is not a motion thesis.

Hold four decisions internally: the **focal moment**, the **continuity** between states, the **feedback** acknowledging actions, and the **budget** for expensive work. Use the prepared brief and design context, keeping effective existing behavior in a refinement.

## Choose a material by meaning

- Explain spatial relationships with shared-element motion, FLIP-style transforms or a deliberate change of depth.
- Reveal composition through masks, clip paths, cropping or controlled occlusion.
- Express material through bounded light, shadow, blur, color or shader changes when appropriate.
- Confirm actions with the smallest clear response; do not repeat the hero's theatre on each control.

Use CSS for declarative states and native JavaScript animation for sequencing, interruption or dynamic values. Scroll should drive an effect only when that relationship matters. Avoid making the entire page depend on a modern API without a usable alternative.

## Rehearse one sequence

Build a clear beginning, a visible focal transformation and a resolved composition. Size the motion for the actual viewport; an imperceptible change is not a delivered focal effect. Use sibling stagger when content appears as a group, keeping the wait bounded. Do not hide every section until a script runs.

Use 100–150ms for immediate feedback, 150–300ms for routine state changes, 300–500ms for layout/overlay transitions and roughly 500–800ms for an authored entrance, adjusting for actual distance and consequence. These are different jobs, not one universal limit. Use confident deceleration; reserve bounce for a material that earns it.

Follow [sequencing and interruption](references/sequencing.md) when actions overlap or the scene has multiple stages. Follow [performance and fallback](references/performance-and-fallback.md) for loops, observers and reduced motion.

## Preserve a complete experience

Keep content visible if initialization fails. Pause nonessential loops when offscreen or the tab is hidden, with a visible pause control when persistent motion is part of the presentation. Under reduced motion, reduce spatial travel while retaining legible feedback and state. Touch and keyboard paths must reach the same content.

The delivered result must show the chosen focal effect as well as useful supporting states. External tests should inspect its timing, interruption, repeated use and device behavior. Do not claim a preview occurred or launch a follow-on polish/review workflow automatically.
