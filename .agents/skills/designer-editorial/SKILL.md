# Preserve meaning and support sustained reading

Choose an index, feature, article or thematic collection according to actual content. Establish title, deck, author/date when provided, and a section hierarchy that represents the argument. Do not compress substantive material into teaser cards just to make the page look short.

Use a comfortable line length, paragraph spacing and typographic distinction between body, captions, quotations, notes and headings. Preserve attribution and qualifiers; a quotation must remain a quotation, and an author's claim is not a verified universal fact.

Place images and videos near the passages they explain, retain supported captions and avoid decorative crops of meaningful material. Use an accessible table of contents for long articles and link related pieces where real destinations exist. Unsupported multi-page publication output should become an honest section index rather than invented URLs.

Build rhythm through section openings, media, comparisons and clear prose hierarchy rather than repeating full-width banners. Use native disclosure sparingly for secondary detail; essential text should remain reachable. Local edits preserve the surrounding text, existing anchors and reading order rather than rewriting the entire article.
