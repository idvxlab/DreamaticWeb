# A concept generates decisions

Borrow a rule system from the subject's world rather than a fashionable appearance. Useful territories include material, audience culture, a real process, name/identity and a formal graphic system. A concept should explain layout, motion, type and imagery together. Its anchor is a supplied fact or a clearly artistic interpretation, not invented company history.

Consider distinct alternatives, then run the swap test: if the entire scene could be transferred to a different company unchanged, its specificity is weak. Choose one governing metaphor and derive several decisions from it. A secondary idea may inform a detail; it should not compete with the main system.

## Composition contract

- Give the first viewport a strong typographic or visual mass, intentional empty space and a clear action.
- Assign palette roles: ground, ink, secondary ink, boundaries and accent. Coordinate colors through motion states, especially at the end of a scroll sequence.
- Choose type by character and real availability. Express hierarchy through size, weight, line breaks, measure and optical alignment; do not rely on an external font that cannot load.
- Give factual tables, narrative, product media and proof different content-appropriate structures.
- Carry the concept past the hero into navigation, section transitions, captions and the closing action. Do not add unrelated pages merely to satisfy a generic website scaffold.

## An authored first encounter

Choose the frame the visitor should remember, then work backward to its entrance. The sequence should reveal the concept, not delay access to the content. There is no reason to invent a loader when no actual resource is loading. Keep navigation usable, the native cursor visible and primary text legible while art direction changes.

On mobile, recompose the focal relationship. Scale detail and travel to the viewport and provide a touch interaction or a deliberate time-driven alternative. A flat reduced-motion version still carries the same hierarchy, palette and material.
