# Give the subject its own visual identity

Work as a design lead with a deliberate point of view. Ground the design in the supplied subject, audience and purpose. Use the prepared brief rather than restarting discovery. Missing business facts remain missing; typography and composition can be expressive without invented credentials, metrics, customers or product capabilities.

Lead with the subject's most characteristic thing: an image, a powerful typographic composition, a live visual or a staged interaction. Choose the form because it makes this subject legible. A big statistic, gradient accent and generic card grid are not a default concept.

## Make coordinated choices

Choose a compact palette with named roles, intentional type voices, a fluid scale, an alignment rule and a section rhythm. Typography is an active visual element: weight, width, line breaks, tracking and negative space create character. Use real available fonts rather than unresolved external font declarations. Keep body text comfortably readable and line length controlled.

Structural devices carry information. Use numbering for a real sequence, borders for a meaningful boundary, and labels for useful context. Do not put all-caps eyebrows, mono metadata or a decorative section number above every heading. Do not make every section the same container with the same corner radius.

Compare the planned direction internally with the category's predictable template and its fashionable opposite. Change the generic part before implementing. Pinned aesthetics from the brief win over a general warning about saturated styles. Follow [composition and typography](references/composition.md) when those decisions need a deeper pass.

## Spend boldness deliberately

One memorable focal treatment can carry the page. For a motion-led marketing brief, give that treatment a visible authored sequence or interactive state, not only repeated fade-ups and hover transitions. Keep surrounding controls quiet, responsive and consistent. An existing page being refined keeps its effective layout, content and unrelated behavior.

Provide mobile reflow, keyboard focus, intentional reduced-motion behavior, readable foreground/background combinations and useful interaction states. Check selector ownership so one broad rule does not cancel spacing or color in another section. Self-check while composing within the existing task; do not create a separate planning or critic workflow.

## Write as the visitor speaks

Use the product's own real language. Actions say what they do. Keep the same name for an action and its resulting state. Errors explain recovery; empty states offer a next action. Remove filler and process narration. The delivered page should be useful without explaining how its content was extracted.
