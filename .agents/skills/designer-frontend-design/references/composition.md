# Coordinated visual decisions

Typography should carry a specific voice. Use a fluid type scale and deliberate line breaks, weights, tracking and body measure. A single strong family can be enough; if using two, give them distinct jobs. Keep the reading measure comfortable rather than filling every wide screen with a full-width paragraph.

Choose the layout from the material: a technical comparison benefits from alignment, a story from sequence and rhythm, work from sufficient image scale. Structural borders, labels and numbering encode relationships. Remove them when they only imitate editorial sophistication.

## Detect the generic move

Look for the identical rounded-card grid, indiscriminate gradients, one accent word in every headline, unnecessary uppercase eyebrows, decorative numbered sections and a theme chosen solely from the industry category. These treatments can belong to a pinned brief; they are weak when they replace a decision. A warm editorial world or a dark technical one is not inherently good or bad—its fit must come from the actual subject.

## One focal gesture

Choose whether the first encounter is image-led, typographic, spatial or interactive. Let a single authored motion reinforce that gesture. Repeated fade-and-slide effects often reduce distinctiveness instead of adding it. Keep quick causal feedback on real actions and preserve the original visual identity during narrow edits.

## Writing and states

Use real material and the visitor's vocabulary. Controls name a concrete action, and the resulting state uses the same term. Errors name the failure and recovery, empty states offer a next step, and marketing copy states supported value. Do not invent client logos, performance metrics or product functions to fill a composition. Make the design work with the actual content density.
