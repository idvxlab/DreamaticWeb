# Connect promise, proof and action

Choose one primary audience and desired next step. Lead with a specific outcome grounded in provided facts, followed by a short explanation of who it is for. Give the primary action a real destination or native anchor. Do not invent testimonials, adoption numbers, partner logos or urgency.

Select a persuasive sequence from the available material: problem and benefit, demonstration, useful comparison, supported evidence, fit/limitations and next step. This is a menu, not a compulsory section template. A sparse source should yield a concise honest page, not fabricated proof or repetitive filler.

Show the product or service using relevant actual media; use captions, examples or process diagrams in HTML/CSS when that explains more than a decorative hero. Keep feature claims specific, including meaningful constraints. Distinguish capabilities from customer outcomes rather than restating the same benefit in several cards.

Give the opening, central explanation and conclusion different visual roles while keeping one design language. Let visitors explore details without losing the action path. Preserve factual qualifiers and prices/terms if supplied. In an existing landing-page edit, update only the requested message, evidence or call to action rather than rerunning the full persuasion sequence.
